# Battery capacity worn

## Summary
::: status Problem
A full charge holds only {{health}} % of the battery's original capacity. Below 80 % a battery is considered at the end of its life, and runtime is clearly shorter.
:::
::: status Ok,Unknown,Info,Unsupported
Compares the capacity of a full charge with the battery's original capacity.
:::

## Why it matters
Every lithium-ion battery loses capacity with charge cycles, heat and time spent at 100 %. Microsoft documents that a battery is typically at the end of its life when its full charge falls below 80 % of its design capacity [1]. No setting can bring the capacity back; the practical options are a new battery or slowing further wear.

## How we detected it
We read the design capacity (`BatteryStaticData.DesignedCapacity`) and the capacity of a full charge (`BatteryFullChargedCapacity.FullChargedCapacity`) that the battery driver reports in milliwatt hours [2][3]. Some drivers report no usable values; the check is then skipped.

## How to fix
1. If runtime is too short for you, ask the laptop manufacturer or a repair shop for a battery replacement.
2. If the laptop is mostly plugged in, turn on the charge limit in the manufacturer's app (often 80 %), which slows further wear.
3. Avoid heat: do not leave the laptop charging in the sun or on soft surfaces that block the vents.

## How to check the fix
Run `powercfg /batteryreport` in a terminal: the report lists design and full charge capacity [4]. After a battery replacement, run the scan again; the health should be close to 100 %.
