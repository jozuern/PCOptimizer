# Throttling under load

## Summary
::: variant cpu
The processor could not hold its full performance in the last measurement: power or heat limits lowered it. Check cooling and power settings.
:::
::: variant gpuThermal
The graphics card reached its temperature limit and lowered its clocks. Check airflow and the card's fans.
:::
::: variant gpuPower
The graphics card ran at its power limit. That is normal under full load and not a fault.
:::
::: variant stale
The last check under load is more than 30 days old. Run it again on the Health page; heat and power limits change with dust, cooling and the season.
:::
::: variant default
Shows the result of the last throttle check under load.
:::

## Why it matters
Processors and graphics cards lower their clock speed when they get too hot or exceed their power budget. In games that shows as lower frame rates and stutter after a few minutes of play, often without any warning.
::: variant gpuPower
Graphics cards are designed to run at their power limit under full load and boost as far as it allows; that alone costs nothing compared to what the card is rated for.
:::

## How we detected it
During the throttle check on the Health page we sampled once per second: the processor's "% Performance Limit" counter of the Processor Information counter set, read through the Windows performance counter interface [1], and, for NVIDIA cards, the driver's clock event reasons, which say whether the clocks are held back to stay within the power limit or below the maximum operating temperature [2]. Microsoft does not publish a detailed definition of "% Performance Limit"; we read a value below 100 as "the processor could not deliver its full nominal performance because of power policy, power budget or heat". Treat a CPU result as a strong hint, not as proof of the cause. Only samples with a busy processor count. A limit in more than 10 % of the samples counts as throttling.

## How to fix
::: variant cpu
1. Use the **Balanced** or a high performance power plan and check the power mode.
::: if laptop
2. Plug in the charger, use the manufacturer's performance mode, and keep the air vents free (not on soft surfaces).
:::
::: ifnot laptop
2. Check the processor cooler: is it mounted firmly, are the fans running, is the heatsink free of dust? Old thermal paste can also be the cause.
3. In the BIOS, check that no low power limit is set (Intel: PL1/PL2, AMD: PPT) unless you set it on purpose. If your system drive uses BitLocker, suspend it before you change BIOS settings [3].
:::
:::
::: variant gpuThermal
1. Clean the graphics card's fans and heatsink, and make sure the case has air flowing in and out.
2. A custom fan curve (for example in MSI Afterburner) keeps the card cooler at the cost of noise.
:::
::: variant gpuPower,default
No action needed.
:::
::: variant stale
Open the Health page and run the throttle check while a game or a load test runs.
:::

## How to check the fix
Run the throttle check again while playing for a few minutes.
